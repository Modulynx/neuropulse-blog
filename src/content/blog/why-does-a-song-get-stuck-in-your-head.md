You're rinsing a mug at the kitchen sink. The radio went off an hour ago. Somehow the chorus of a song you didn't even like is still playing, on a loop, in your head.

It's a small thing, but it's hard to ignore. When a **song is stuck in your head**, you can't find the off switch, and it can wear on you by mid-afternoon.

So why does it happen, and is there anything you can do about it?

## Why does a song get stuck in your head?

Short answer: your brain is doing its normal job a little too well.

Your auditory cortex handles every sound you hear. Voices, traffic, the microwave beeping, and music. It doesn't only react when sound reaches your ears. It can also keep going on its own, especially with music you know well.

That's why a familiar song can keep playing after the sound is gone. It's common, and it doesn't mean something is wrong with you.

## What's happening in your brain when a song loops?

Think of your phone's autocomplete. You type two words, and it offers the third before you ask. Your brain does something similar with songs you know. It has the whole tune stored, so when part of it goes missing, it fills in the rest.

That's the idea behind the study below. If the brain only responded to sound, silence should mean quiet. In a familiar song, it doesn't seem to.

## What did the research find?

In 2005, Kraemer and colleagues published a brain-imaging study in the journal Nature. They looked at what happened when short silent gaps were placed inside familiar songs.

The auditory cortex was still active during those gaps. In other words, the brain filled in the silence itself.

That's a useful clue about stuck songs. Your brain can keep "playing" music with nothing coming in from outside.

Two honest limits. This study looked at silent gaps in familiar music, not at stuck songs directly. So it helps explain why a song can keep going in your head, but it doesn't tell us why one song sticks and another doesn't.

The video this post is based on also mentions a separate study in which chewing gum reduced stuck songs. We don't have the details to share here, so take it as a hint worth trying, not a promise.

## What can you try today?

The habit: **give your brain something else to do, and let your mouth be busy too.** Chewing gum is the easy version of that. Research on this is limited, so think of it as a small experiment, not a fix.

Here's a simple three-step plan for today:

1. **Notice it without a fight.** Tell yourself, "Oh, there's that song again." Pushing hard against a tune tends to keep it in the spotlight.
2. **Chew some gum.** Sugar-free is a kind choice for your teeth. Keep it going while you do the next step.
3. **Pick one small task that needs words or attention.** Answer an email, read a page, sort the mail on the counter. Something that takes real focus works better than something you can do on autopilot.

If you want a structure for the focus part, our post on the [90 minute focus block](/blog/90-minute-focus-block) is a good next read.

## Who should be careful?

Gum is fine for most adults, but skip it if you have jaw pain, TMJ problems, or dental work that gum could damage. Don't give gum to young children because of the choking risk.

If you're pregnant, on medication, or have a heart condition, you don't need to change anything about your health for this. Still, check with your doctor before changing habits that involve caffeine or nicotine gum, since those versions contain active ingredients.

If songs or thoughts loop so much that they cause distress, get in the way of daily life, or come with anxiety, talk with a doctor or mental health professional. The same goes if you ever hear music that isn't really there and it doesn't feel like a normal memory loop.

## FAQ

### Why does a song get stuck in your head?

Your auditory cortex handles all the sound you hear, and it can stay active even when nothing is playing. A 2005 brain-imaging study by Kraemer and colleagues in Nature found that silent gaps in familiar songs still activated this area, so the brain fills in the silence itself. It's a normal brain habit, not a sign of a problem.

### Does chewing gum help get a song out of your head?

It may help. One study mentioned in our source video found that chewing gum reduced stuck songs. We don't have more detail than that, so it's worth a try but not a guaranteed fix.

### Is it normal to have a song stuck in your head all day?

Yes, it's very common, and it usually passes by itself. If the looping feels distressing, gets in the way of your day, or comes with anxiety, it's a good idea to talk with a doctor or mental health professional.
