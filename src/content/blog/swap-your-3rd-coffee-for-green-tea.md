It's 2:30 in the afternoon. The third coffee is half gone, your hands feel a little buzzy, and you've read the same email line four times.

You're awake. You're just not focused. So what could you drink instead? This is where green tea instead of coffee is worth a try.

## Why does your third coffee leave you wired?

Caffeine is thought to work by blocking adenosine, a chemical that builds up during the day and makes you feel sleepy. With adenosine out of the way, you feel alert.

But alert isn't the same as steady. After a few cups, many people feel jumpy, restless or scattered. It's a bit like a radio turned up so loud that the static comes through with the music.

So the problem isn't always a lack of alertness. Sometimes it's too much of it, with no calm underneath.

## What does green tea do that coffee doesn't?

Green tea has caffeine too, but usually less per cup than coffee. It also contains L-theanine, an amino acid found in tea leaves.

Research suggests L-theanine may take some of the edge off caffeine. The idea is that you get the alertness with a smoother feel, instead of the wired kind.

That's a hypothesis about how it feels, and we can't see inside your head. Your own cup may land differently.

## What does the research say?

A 2008 study in the journal Nutritional Neuroscience looked at L-theanine and caffeine together. It found that the pair can support calmer and steadier attention.

That's the part we can say with some confidence. We can't say how big the effect is for you, or exactly how it works in the brain. It's one study, so treat it as a reason to experiment, not a promise.

It also doesn't mean coffee is bad. If you want a look at the other side, we wrote about how [one black coffee before focused work may sharpen reactions](/blog/one-black-coffee-before-focused-work-may-sharpen-reactions). The two ideas fit together. One cup can be useful, and the third is where the jitters tend to creep in.

## How do you try green tea instead of coffee today?

The habit is simple: when you reach for your third coffee, make a mug of green tea instead.

Here's a mini plan for today:

1. **Pick your swap moment.** Decide now that coffee number three becomes tea. Put a tea bag next to the mug you'd normally use for coffee, so the choice is already made.
2. **Make it slowly.** Heat the water, steep the tea, and sip it warm. Taking a few minutes is part of the point, since you're stepping away from the screen for a moment.
3. **Notice how you feel.** After the mug, check in. Are you more settled, about the same, or no different? Jot it down in a few words so you can compare tomorrow.

If you don't like the taste at first, try a lighter brew or let it cool a little. Tea that's steeped too long can turn bitter.

## When might this not help?

Green tea still has caffeine, so a late-afternoon mug can get in the way of sleep if you're sensitive to it. If you notice that, try it earlier in the day.

It also won't help everyone feel calmer. If you often get a racing heart, constant jitteriness or trouble sleeping, even with little caffeine, talk to a doctor. The same goes for anxiety that lasts or gets worse. A mug of tea is a small experiment, not a treatment.

## FAQ

### Does green tea have less caffeine than coffee?

Usually, yes, per cup. The exact amount depends on the tea, how long you steep it and the size of your mug. Coffee varies a lot too, so it's a general pattern, not a rule.

### Is green tea better than coffee for focus?

Not necessarily better, but it may feel smoother for some people. A 2008 study in Nutritional Neuroscience found that L-theanine plus caffeine can support calmer, steadier attention. It's one study, so it's worth testing for yourself.

### What is L-theanine and where does it come from?

L-theanine is an amino acid found naturally in tea leaves. A cup of green tea contains some of it. If you're thinking about supplements instead, ask a pharmacist or doctor first, since they work differently from a mug of tea.
