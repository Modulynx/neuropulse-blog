It's 8:40 on a Tuesday night. The textbook is open, the highlighter is uncapped, and your phone is face down next to your mug. It hasn't buzzed once. You still read the same paragraph three times.

One small study tested exactly this: a phone that's off, sitting right there.

## Why does your focus slip when the phone is right there?

Behind your forehead sits the part of your brain that holds your goal and keeps you on task. Call it your planning center.

With the phone in reach, that planning center may have an extra job: not picking it up. It's like a browser tab you never opened but can still feel in the background.

<!-- img:brain -->

## What did the study find?

Researchers at Paderborn University in Germany gave 42 college students a standard attention test (the d2-R) over video calls ([Skowronek et al., Scientific Reports, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-36256-4)). Every phone was switched off. Half the students put theirs face down on the desk. The other half left theirs outside the room.

The phone-outside group scored about 109 on attention, against about 100 for the phone-on-desk group. They also worked faster, with speed scores of about 109 against about 98.

## What this study can't tell us

It was one short test with 42 young, mostly highly educated students, run from home over video calls. It can't say how long the effect lasts over a real study night. It also didn't test a phone in a bag or a phone on silent, so we can't compare those.

## What's one habit to try tonight?

1. **Switch it off before you sit down.** Fully off.
2. **Put it in another room.** The kitchen counter or a bag in the hallway.
3. **Pick a stopping point.** The end of a chapter. Check your phone then, not before.

<!-- img:fix -->

If you use your phone as a timer, use a cheap kitchen timer instead. The first ten minutes may feel itchy. That passes.

## Who should be careful?

If you're on call, caring for someone, or need your phone for health alerts, keep a way to be reached. If being unreachable makes you anxious, start with a short stretch and tell someone where you'll be.

## FAQ

### Should I leave my phone in another room while studying?

In the study above, students whose switched-off phone was outside the room did better on an attention test than students with a switched-off phone on the desk. It's one small study, so treat it as something to try.

### Does a phone on silent still distract you?

This study didn't test silent mode. Both groups had their phones switched off; only the location changed.

### What can I use instead of my phone as a timer?

A kitchen timer, a basic watch or a desk clock.
